# JEOPARDY · DAY 2 — Lessons 2 & 3 Review (DRAFT)

**Format:** 6 categories × 5 clues each = 30 clues. Values $100–$500 (easy → hard). One Daily Double (marked ⭐).

**Coverage:**
- **Lessons 2**: Claim / Warrant / Impact (the "argument sandwich")
- **Lesson 3**: Structure of a policy debate round — 8 speeches, constructive vs. rebuttal, CX, AFF/NEG roles

**Jeopardy convention:** clues are written as **statements/answers**; students respond in the **form of a question** ("What is...?" / "Who is...?"). Feel free to relax that rule for novices.

---

## Category 1 — THE ARGUMENT SANDWICH 🥪 (Lesson 2)

| $ | Clue | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | This is the top bun of the argument sandwich — your big idea in one clear sentence. | What is a **CLAIM**? |
| $200 | This is the filling — the reason or evidence that shows why your claim is true. | What is a **WARRANT**? |
| $300 | This is the bottom bun — the part that answers "so what? why does it matter?" | What is an **IMPACT**? |
| $400 | "Pizza is the best food." According to Lesson 2, this argument is missing these two parts. | What are the **warrant and impact**? |
| $500 | A claim without a warrant is just this; a warrant without an impact is just this. | What are **an opinion / a fun fact**? |

## Category 2 — SPEECH ORDER 📋 (Lesson 3)

| $ | Clue | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | This is the very first speech of any policy debate round. | What is the **1AC** (First Affirmative Constructive)? |
| $200 | This is the very last speech of the round — and the team that gives it. | What is the **2AR**, given by the **affirmative**? |
| $300 | After every constructive speech, this 3-minute Q&A period happens. | What is **cross-examination (CX)**? |
| $400 | Name all four **constructive** speeches in order. | What are **1AC, 1NC, 2AC, 2NC**? |
| ⭐$500 | Name all four **rebuttal** speeches in order. | What are **1NR, 1AR, 2NR, 2AR**? |

## Category 3 — CONSTRUCTIVE vs. REBUTTAL 🏗️⚖️ (Lesson 3)

| $ | Clue | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | This phase of the round is where you BUILD your arguments and read evidence. | What is a **constructive** speech? |
| $200 | This phase is where you DEFEND your case and ATTACK your opponent's — no new arguments. | What is a **rebuttal** speech? |
| $300 | Lesson 3 compares constructives to this job — someone who builds a house brick by brick. | Who is a **construction worker**? |
| $400 | Lesson 3 compares rebuttals to this job — someone who defends their case in court. | Who is a **lawyer**? |
| $500 | This is what you generally CANNOT do in a rebuttal speech that you CAN do in a constructive. | What is **read new evidence / make new arguments**? |

## Category 4 — KNOW YOUR POSITION 🎽 (Lesson 3)

| $ | Clue | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | This team argues that the plan is a good idea. | Who is the **Affirmative (AFF)**? |
| $200 | This team argues that the plan is a bad idea. | Who is the **Negative (NEG)**? |

## Category 5 — BUILD IT / BREAK IT 🔨 (Lesson 2 applied)

For each mini-argument, students identify which part is present or missing.

| $ | Clue | Correct Response |
|---|---|---|
| $100 | "Schools should keep recess **because studies show students focus better after physical activity**." Which part is the bolded piece? | What is the **warrant**? |
| $200 | "Uniforms hurt student creativity, **which means less engagement and worse grades**." Which part is the bolded piece? | What is the **impact**? |
| $300 | "Homework is bad." What's missing from this argument? | What are the **warrant and impact**? |
| $400 | "82% of students express themselves through their clothes." This statement is just a warrant — it needs THIS and THIS to become a complete argument. | What are a **claim and an impact**? |
| $500 | You say: "Recess should stay because kids focus better afterwards, which means higher test scores." Label all three parts in order. | What are **CLAIM (recess should stay) / WARRANT (kids focus better) / IMPACT (higher test scores)**? |

## Notes for Austin

- **Daily Double is marked ⭐** on Category 2, $500 (all four rebuttals in order). Move it wherever fits your class dynamic — a good DD is hard but earnable, and lands mid-game so scoreboards matter. Reasonable alternates: Cat 5 $500 (label all three parts) or Cat 3 $500 (new evidence in rebuttals).
- **Difficulty ramp:** every category is written easy → hard. If your kids are stronger than expected, bump the $100/$200 clues into "recall the exact acronym" territory (e.g., "This stands for First Negative Constructive").
- **Wrong-answer traps** I deliberately baked in:
  - Cat 4 $400: kids will guess "1A" (last speech is the 2AR, given by 2A)
  - Cat 3 $500: this is a classic novice mistake — reinforces the "no new args in rebuttals" rule
  - Cat 5 $100: kids often mislabel the "because" clause; this drills it
- **What I did NOT include:** the "Mars in a Box" activity from Lesson 2 (fun but not a testable concept), the specific "1AC → 1NC → …" arrow diagram (would need a visual clue), and any Lesson 2.5 vocab (that's separate).
- **Length check:** at ~90 seconds/clue with team play, 30 clues = ~45 min. Add 10 min setup/wrap = single class period.
